Between shifts: a gig worker's day, stacked

Maya is 24. She drives deliveries between classes, and her day is full of small gaps — waiting for an order, waiting for a pickup, waiting for the next ping. Those minutes used to vanish. This is the story of how she started filling them on purpose, with three small earners that fit the day she already has. Watch her day, then borrow the parts that fit yours.

Maya's day, hour by hour

7:40 a.m. — the first wait. The restaurant is running behind on her first pickup. Instead of scrolling, Maya opens a survey she qualified for last night and finishes it before the bag is ready. Her rule: profile surveys first, because completed profiles are what get matched to studies instead of screened out.

12:15 p.m. — the grocery stop she was making anyway. Between orders she buys her own groceries. At the car, she scans the receipt before she drives off — thirty seconds, done while the trip is fresh.

6:30 p.m. — shopping she had already planned. She needs new shoes for work. She starts through a rewards site, buys the same shoes at the same store, and the purchase earns points back on top.

10:00 p.m. — the honest tally. None of the three paid like a shift. Together, across a week of gaps she was spending anyway, they behaved like one extra small shift — without adding an hour to her day.

Steal Maya's rules, not her schedule

  • Only fill gaps that already exist. The stack works because it costs no extra time — the moment it needs its own hour, it stops being a gap-filler.
  • One layer at a time. Start with surveys for a week. Add receipts when that habit sticks. Rewards last.
  • Scan receipts the same day. A receipt in a glove box is a rebate you already forgot.
  • Keep the ceiling honest. Surveys and rewards are pocket money, never shift pay — anyone promising full-time income from panels is selling something.
  • Skip anything that charges you to join. Legitimate panels are free; they are paid by brands, not by you.
